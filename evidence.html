What I measured: 30 AI answers, 3 engines, 2 brands

Ten prompts, run against ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews. Once against a tool almost nobody has heard of, once against one with a decade of public reviews. Same prompts, same day, same country.

Two things fall out of that. First, the measurement is real: the same prompts separated a brand the engines know from one they do not, which is what you would expect and what a noisy test would not produce. Second, and more usefully — being named is not being cited. The established tool was named in 26 of 29 answers and linked in only 8. Where it was named without a link, the sources shown pointed at G2, Capterra and Product Hunt — the review sites, not the product.

This is a snapshot: one day, one country, two brands, three engines, 29 usable answers per brand. It is not a market study, and nothing below is a trend.

Three captures from the run

Three of the 30 captures per brand; 60 screenshots in total were kept as the audit trail. Scoring sheets, one row per check, with the answer-level note: first run (OmniSEO) · second run (Plausible).

Method, exactly as run

  • 10 fixed prompts, wording unchanged between brands, covering discovery, alternatives and evaluation.
  • 3 engines: ChatGPT (logged out, web search on), Perplexity (free plan, Search mode), Google AI Overviews (fresh incognito context per prompt, logged out, gl=ke).
  • One new session per prompt. The first answer was recorded. Prompts were not re-run in the hope of a better result.
  • Two fields per check: was the brand named, and was it linked to its own domain. A citation to G2 or Product Hunt counts as named, not linked.
  • Capture was automated through the browser — typed, submitted, answer text and screenshot saved per check. It was not done by hand, and nothing here claims it was.

Deviations, in full

  1. ChatGPT was logged out — no memory, and not the paid tier.
  2. Perplexity was on a free plan. Paid tiers can behave differently.
  3. One check per run is n/a: a Google AI Overview never rendered (prompt 6 first run, prompt 1 second run). Recorded as not applicable rather than as a miss, so each denominator is 29.
  4. Two ChatGPT checks in the second run returned no text on the first pass because of a capture bug, not an empty answer (prompts 9 and 10). They were re-captured once with a fixed extractor and the first captured answer was kept.
  5. “Linked” is strict: an anchor to the brand's own domain. Answers that named a brand and cited G2, Capterra or Product Hunt are counted as named, not linked.
  6. For AI Overviews, a looser flag fired on two checks in the second run while no anchor survived in the saved page. Those are reported as not linked, which is the conservative reading.

What this does not tell you

It does not say which tool is better, which brand will grow, or how AI answers will behave next month. It says that on one day, in one country, a known brand and an unknown brand were treated very differently by the same three engines on the same ten questions — and that being named and being given a link are two different things.

Run it on your own brand

This needs no software. Ten prompts, three engines, one afternoon. Replace the brackets and record the first answer each time.

  1. What's the best [category] for [use case]?
  2. Best [category] tools for small teams in 2026
  3. Which [category] would you recommend for [use case]?
  4. What are the best alternatives to [competitor]?
  5. [competitor] vs the rest — what else should I look at before I switch?
  6. I don't want to use [competitor]. What should I use instead?
  7. Is [brand] actually any good?
  8. What do people say about [brand]?
  9. [brand] reviews — pros and cons
  10. Should I buy [brand] or [competitor]?

Score two things per answer: named and linked to your own domain. If an engine refuses or an AI Overview does not render, mark it n/a and leave it out of the denominator. Ten prompts × three engines = 30 checks; you should finish with 27–30 usable ones.
