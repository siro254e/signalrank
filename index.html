Why "AI visibility" is a real category now

ChatGPT answers product questions directly, without a list of blue links. When someone asks it "best project management tool for agencies", it names two or three tools and says why. If you're not one of them, that person never sees your pricing page.

Search sent you rankings. AI answers send you citations — and the two are not the same signal. A brand can rank well in Google and be absent from the ChatGPT answer to the same question, because the models draw on different sources and summarise rather than link.

That is why nearly every serious SEO platform now ships an "AI visibility" module. The question for a buyer is whether that module is a checkbox on a feature list or a product with its own data pipeline.

The others — what each plan is actually for

Promptwatch ($95/mo Essential) is the only one here that pairs visibility with crawler logs and AI-referred conversion tracking — the "are these bots real and do they convert" question. The $95 tier allows one project, 50 prompts and 6,000 responses a month; Professional is $245 for 2 projects and 150 prompts, Business $579 for 5 projects and 350. Every tier tracks all supported models, and MCP plus API access are included even on entry, which is unusual at this price.

AthenaHQ (free, then $295/mo Starter) is the strongest plan on paper and the one to beat OmniSEO against. The free tier gives 300 credits across five models; Starter gives 3,600 credits, 11 models, unlimited prompts and topics, unlimited competitors, CSV export and unlimited seats. The catch is right there in the unit: one credit is one AI response. Track 50 prompts across 11 models and you have spent 550 credits per refresh — roughly six refreshes a month. The price is low because the allowance is finite, so work out your prompt volume before assuming $295 beats $349.

Scrunch AI ($250/mo Core) is the narrowest of the five and priced accordingly: 125 unique prompts, 5,000 responses a month, four models (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews, Copilot), five competitors, five seats, and five site audits a month. The nine-model tier is Enterprise-only. If your problem is making pages legible to AI crawlers rather than benchmarking a market, the audit and Agent Pages tooling is the differentiator; if you need ten engines, this is not the plan.

LLM Pulse (€49/mo Starter) is the value pick and the easiest one to defend on a tight budget. Fifty tracked prompts, one project, five models included (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Mode, AI Overviews, Gemini), ten competitors, CSV/Excel/PDF export on every tier, unlimited team members and an MCP server at €49. Extra models are paid add-ons; Growth is €99 for 150 prompts and two projects, Scale €299 for 450 prompts and five.

One caveat that applies to all five: "engines covered" is a vendor claim, not a measured accuracy figure. Ask each vendor how often a prompt is re-sampled before you buy — that cadence, not the engine count, determines whether the tool catches a change while it still matters.

Five things that matter more than the engine count

  1. A citation profile is not a ranking profile. Ranking well in Google does not put you in the ChatGPT answer to the same question, because the two systems draw on different sources. If you assume your rankings transfer, you will skip the measurement entirely and be wrong about it. This is the core reason the category exists.
  2. The unit on the pricing page is the real price. These five tools meter capacity four different ways: saved prompts (OmniSEO), agent credits (Promptwatch), AI responses (AthenaHQ), responses per month (Scrunch), tracked prompts with paid model add-ons (LLM Pulse). A $295 plan that meters 3,600 responses is not cheaper than a $349 plan with 100 saved prompts until you work out how often you want each prompt re-run. Do that arithmetic before the trial ends, not after.
  3. Comparison prompts and evaluation prompts behave differently. "Best X for Y" tends to return the biggest names; "is X actually good" is where smaller brands get named. Most tools let you choose prompts, so build both types into your set rather than accepting a default list.
  4. Freshness of your own pages matters. Sources cited in AI answers skew toward recently updated pages. That makes content refresh a visibility tactic, not just an SEO housekeeping task — and it is something you can act on without buying any of these tools.
  5. None of these tools will fix bad content. They tell you where you are absent and whether a change moved the number. The writing, the structure and the source-worthiness are still yours. A visibility tool that reports a problem you cannot fix is an expensive way to feel informed.

Who should not buy this

  • Solo bloggers under ~50 pages. You do not need $349/mo. Use a free trial of the cheapest option and re-evaluate in a quarter.
  • Anyone whose customers don't ask AI for recommendations. Check whether this is actually your buying path before you buy a tool for it. For most B2C products it still isn't.
  • Teams who need to watch more than five competitors on OmniSEO. Its cap is five on every tier, including Enterprise. AthenaHQ publishes no cap, Promptwatch publishes none, LLM Pulse allows 10 to 20 per project, and Scrunch's is five unless you go Enterprise. If competitor benchmarking is the job, that single number should decide your shortlist.
