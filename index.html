Why "AI visibility" is a real category now

ChatGPT answers product questions directly, without a list of blue links. When someone asks it "best project management tool for agencies", it names two or three tools and says why. If you're not one of them, that person never sees your pricing page.

Search sent you rankings. AI answers send you citations — and the two are not the same signal. A brand can rank #3 for a keyword in Google and be completely absent from the ChatGPT answer to the same question, because the models cite different sources.

"Platforms are getting better at ranking real engagement over raw view counts, and the same is happening in search: being mentioned in the answer is the new ranking."

That's why every serious SEO tool now ships an "AI Visibility" module. The question is whether that module is a checkbox or an actual product.

How I tested

  • Site: a live 6-figure-revenue ecommerce brand I have access to (12 product pages, 40+ existing backlinks, real traffic) — not a throwaway test domain.
  • Prompt set: 40 prompts across 3 categories — "best X for Y", "alternatives to Z", and "is Z any good". The third type is where most tools under-report, and where the interesting data was.
  • Engines: ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Copilot, Claude, Google AI Overview, Google AI Mode, Meta AI, Grok, DeepSeek.
  • Method: ran every tool's own prompt sampler at the same prompts, then verified each "citation" by hand. Two tools claimed citations that were not in the actual answer when I checked. Both are named below.
  • Duration: 30 days, to catch the week-to-week churn in model answers. AI answers are volatile; one snapshot is not evidence.

What I actually learned in 30 days

  1. Your citation profile is not your ranking profile. The brand that ranked #3 for our target keyword appeared in 1 of 10 ChatGPT answers. A competitor ranking #11 appeared in 4 of 10. Rank tracking would never have shown you that.
  2. The "is X any good" prompt is the one that matters. Prompts of the form "best X for Y" get answered with the biggest names. Prompts of the form "is X actually good" are where smaller brands get mentioned — and where most tools sample least.
  3. Freshness dominates. The sources cited most often were pages updated within 90 days. Our best-performing page by AI citations was the one we had refreshed most recently, by accident, three weeks before the test.
  4. Ten engines is a real advantage for teams, and irrelevant for individuals. ChatGPT and AI Overviews covered ~80% of what I saw. The other eight mattered only when I was reporting to a client who asked "and what does Claude say?"
  5. None of these tools will fix bad content. Every low-citation page we had was low-citation for a reason. The tool tells you where you are absent; it does not make you present.

Who should not buy this

  • Solo bloggers under ~50 pages. You do not need $349/mo. Use a free trial of the cheapest option and re-evaluate in a quarter.
  • Anyone whose customers don't ask AI for recommendations. Check whether this is actually your buying path before you buy a tool for it. For most B2C products it still isn't.
  • Teams who need more than 5 competitors tracked. Every plan I tested capped this at five. If you need twenty, none of these are the right tool.
