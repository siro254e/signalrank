The verdict in one paragraph

OmniSEO is the right buy if you are an agency or in-house team that has to show someone else the answer. Professional at $349/mo is the only tier with data export, and export is the line between “we looked at it” and “we reported on it”. It tracks more engines than anything else in this comparison — ten, against four on Essentials.

It is the wrong buy if competitor benchmarking is the job. Every tier caps competitor tracking at five, including Enterprise. AthenaHQ publishes no cap, LLM Pulse allows 10–20 per project, Promptwatch publishes none. And the seven-day trial only covers ChatGPT and Google AI Overviews — two of the ten engines you are paying for.

Essentials ($89/mo) — visibility without evidence

Four channels: Google AI Overviews, Google AI Mode, ChatGPT and Perplexity. Fifty saved prompts a month, five competitors, five seats. No export on this tier — you can read the dashboard, but you cannot take the data into a client report.

Professional ($349/mo) — the reporting tier

Adds the other six channels (Gemini, Copilot, Claude, Meta AI, Grok, DeepSeek), 100 saved prompts, ten seats, and CSV/PDF export capped at 5,000 rows a month. This is the plan the rest of this review is about, because it is the only one where the output can leave the tool.

Enterprise ($899+/mo) — seats and JSON

JSON export, unlimited seats, and the five-competitor cap restated as five per brand or service line. If you need more than five competitors for one brand, the plan you need does not exist.

The limits that actually decide the price

1. The trial covers two of ten engines

The seven-day trial does not require a card, which is genuinely useful. But it only exposes ChatGPT and Google AI Overviews. You cannot evaluate Gemini, Copilot, Claude, Meta AI, Grok or DeepSeek — six of the ten engines you are buying on Professional — before you pay. If those engines are the reason you are switching, the trial cannot de-risk that decision.

2. Five competitors, on every tier

Five is the weakest number in OmniSEO’s plan grid relative to the field. AthenaHQ is unlimited, LLM Pulse is 10–20, Promptwatch publishes no cap. A local agency watching ten client competitors hits the ceiling on day one, and no upgrade fixes it.

3. 100 saved prompts is the real meter

Professional allows 100 saved prompts a month. At the full $349 that is $3.49 per saved prompt per month; Essentials works out to $1.78 at $89. The headline price is not the cost — the cost is per prompt you actually monitor. Ten clients at ten prompts each is the whole allowance with nothing left over.

4. Export is gated, and capped

CSV and PDF arrive only at $349, capped at 5,000 rows a month. JSON is Enterprise-only. If your workflow ends in Looker, BigQuery or a client portal, price the row cap before you commit.

5. AI Bot Analytics is a platform capability, not a line item

OmniSEO lists AI Bot Analytics — which AI crawlers hit your site and what they requested — as a platform capability rather than a per-tier feature. Confirm it is on your plan if that is the deciding reason you are buying.

Where it is genuinely strong

  • Ten engines on Professional. The widest published count among the five tools compared here; AthenaHQ reaches eleven only on its top configuration, and the others sit at four to nine.
  • Export at the mid tier. Several competitors gate data out at Enterprise. OmniSEO gates it at $349, which is the tier an agency actually buys.
  • Ten seats on Professional. Enough for a small account team without buying per-head.
  • AI-referred crawler data. Seeing which bots read your pages is a different signal from seeing where you are named, and few plans in this price band publish it.
  • Honest trial terms. No card for seven days, and 50% off the first paid month.

Who should not buy it

  • Solo bloggers under ~50 pages. $89/mo for four engines is more than the problem is worth; use a free trial and revisit in a quarter.
  • Anyone who needs to watch more than five competitors. The cap is structural, not a plan limit you can upgrade past.
  • Teams that must validate all ten engines before paying. The trial will not show you six of them.
  • Buyers who need a free tier. There isn’t one. The trial is the free part.
  • Anyone who has not checked whether AI answers name them at all. Run the free test first — ten prompts across three engines, one hour, no software. If the brand never appears, a monitoring tool will only confirm the absence. The 5-method guide and 10-prompt set walks through it.

Why any of this matters: the 30-answer test

Before comparing tools, I ran the measurement myself: ten buyer-intent prompts across ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews, 30 answers, one brand. Twenty-nine answers were usable. The brand was named in 12 of 29. Only 4 of 29 included a link to its own site. On the 17 unbranded prompts, it appeared zero times.

FAQ

Is there a free trial?

Yes — seven days, no card required. It covers ChatGPT and Google AI Overviews only.

Does Essentials include export?

No. CSV/PDF export starts at Professional ($349/mo), capped at 5,000 rows a month. JSON is Enterprise-only.

How many AI engines does it track?

Four on Essentials (Google AI Overviews, Google AI Mode, ChatGPT, Perplexity) and ten on Professional (adds Gemini, Copilot, Claude, Meta AI, Grok, DeepSeek).

Can I track more than five competitors?

No. Five on every tier; Enterprise restates it as five per brand or service line.

Is annual billing cheaper?

Two months free: $890 for Essentials, $3,490 for Professional. The first paid month after the trial is 50% off.

Does it replace an SEO tool?

No. Rank trackers tell you where you rank; this tells you whether an AI names you. They answer different questions and neither replaces the other.
